# Search visibility baseline and research — 2026-11-27

This is a dated, qualitative discovery audit. It records public-site evidence and representative search results; it does not contain Search Console or Bing private metrics, search-volume estimates, verified Google/Bing ranking positions, or AI citation results.

## Evidence collected

- Screened 52 English, Persian and mixed-language query strings through an integrated web search surface across document parsing/OCR, roadmaps, careers, vector databases, RAG, LLM foundations, agents, evaluation and inference. Search-result pages are not a substitute for region/device-specific Google or Bing rank tracking.
- Opened and reviewed 10 representative content, product or research pages. Six were competing or adjacent content/product sources; the rest were benchmarks, primary research or a provider guide that did not render readable text.
- Mapped all 19 published article routes in [`keyword-page-map.csv`](keyword-page-map.csv). Query phrases are candidates based on current manuscripts and observed result types, not measured query demand.
- The existing [`ai_visibility_queries.csv`](../ai_visibility_queries.csv) contains 39 manual benchmark prompts. The matching observations CSV remains empty; it is a protocol, not evidence of citations.

## Search-result observations and opportunities

### Persian Document Parsing and OCR

Searches around `پردازش اسناد فارسی`, `پردازش PDF فارسی برای RAG`, `استخراج جدول از PDF فارسی` and mixed English variants surfaced both product explainers and open-source projects. [Aria AI's Persian OCR comparison](https://aria-ai.ir/knowledge-base/persian-ocr-comparison/) explicitly treats joined Persian letters, نیم‌فاصله, Persian digits, mixed RTL/LTR text and offline operation as evaluation concerns. Its comparison is self-published by a commercial provider, so its tool results are not independent benchmark evidence. Its [Persian table extraction guide](https://aria-ai.ir/knowledge-base/pdf-table-extraction-persian/) separates text-layer PDFs from scanned pages and calls out RTL columns, Persian/Arabic digits, merged cells and validation. [ParsRAG](https://github.com/Pooria82/ParsRAG) is an adjacent open-source Persian/English local-RAG project that describes local OCR and cited answers; its repository claims are not independently evaluated here.

The v7 Persian Document Parsing article is the site's strongest topic-specific page and already has the canonical `/fa/writing/document-parsing/` route. Keep it distinct from the retired English tutorial. Preserve its production pipeline, route decisions and limitations; future edits should link original tool documentation and publish reproducible evaluations rather than repeat commercial comparison claims.

### AI Engineering roadmaps and careers

[Bardia's Persian roadmap](https://bardia.ai/ai-course/) is an extensive 52-week curriculum with staged study, projects and a local progress UI. [aiArch's English roadmap](https://aiarch.dev/ai-engineer-roadmap) organizes skills into nine stages and ends each stage with a runnable artifact. These pages set reader expectations for sequence, prerequisites and concrete outputs. The publication's compact roadmap is useful for orientation, but its short length and time estimates do not compete as a full syllabus. Its strongest sustainable role is a navigational hub linking the existing foundations, RAG, evaluation, agent and production pages.

[Rahtakar's Persian AI Engineer career guide](https://rahtakar.com/job/ai-machine-learning/ai-engineer) combines job duties, routes into the profession, current salary/job-posting claims and role comparisons. Its displayed counts are the site's own research, not independently validated here. The publication's market-analysis pages can distinguish themselves through transparent date, geography, sample and method notes, not by copying current numerical claims.

### Vector database comparison

[Layerbase's 2026 vector database comparison](https://layerbase.com/blog/vector-databases-compared-2026) leads with a decision tree and practical workload cases, but it also promotes its own cloud product and makes scale/performance claims. Treat these as vendor claims. The publication's existing comparison explicitly warns that its inherited benchmark conditions and product/license descriptions are not independently verified. That disclosure is valuable; the page still needs a source- and version-aware refresh before it can serve as current product guidance. A future independent comparison should state workload, dataset, embedding model, version, hardware, filtering, recall/latency percentiles, memory and cost, or avoid implying a measured winner.

An [empirical ANN database study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12812) reports a multi-system, multi-dataset methodology with quality, latency, throughput and resource metrics. Its reported workloads are not a direct proxy for Persian RAG production, and the preprint results should not be copied as site-owned benchmarks. It is a useful methodology reference.

### Persian embedding and retrieval opportunity

The [FaMTEB paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.614/) establishes Persian embedding evaluation as a concrete research topic, and the [PTEB leaderboard](https://theaggregate.ai/benchmark?slug=pteb) exposes a Persian embedding benchmark view with a dated data snapshot. This suggests a future Persian retrieval/embedding evaluation article, but only if the publication can document dataset versions, licensing, model revisions, task setup and reproducible results. No result from these sources is attributed to the author.

### Interview and foundational tutorials

The English and Persian interview articles cover a broad set of LLM engineering topics. Their differentiator should be technically sound, authored answer rubrics and implementation scenarios if the owner chooses to add them; a query list alone is not unique evidence. Seven short English foundation tutorials currently have descriptions that repeat their page titles. Their descriptions are corrected in the accompanying change. The articles themselves remain brief outlines; the keyword map marks them for future editorial depth rather than presenting thin outlines as comprehensive tutorials.

| IndexNow for changed/new/removed URLs | `OFFICIALLY_SUPPORTED` by the [protocol](https://www.indexnow.org/documentation) | Use the existing post-deployment workflow for content changes. HTTP 202 means key validation is pending; it is not indexing confirmation. |
| Standard SEO for Google AI Overviews and AI Mode | `OFFICIALLY_SUPPORTED` by [Google's AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) | Use accessible, useful, sourced articles; no AI-only schema or `llms.txt` is required by Google. |
| Useful related internal links, topic hubs, clear headings | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_UNPROVEN` as direct ranking gains; crawlable navigation is supported as discovery practice | Improve where it helps readers and navigation. Do not optimize for link count alone. |
| Matching every query phrase in headings/descriptions | `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_UNPROVEN` and potentially `HARMFUL` if forced | Use natural language and distinct page intent. No keyword density targets. |
| `llms.txt` as a search-ranking or citation switch | `UNPROVEN`; Google says special AI text files are not needed | Not implemented. Reconsider only if a relevant provider documents an actual use case. |
| Hidden text, fake schema, purchased links, artificial visits | `HARMFUL` under [Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) | Prohibited. |

## Production and provider status

The pre-change production crawl used Pages deployment commit `af1479554b379ae498d07ca570057e38dc920a43`. It crawled 75 same-host URLs seeded from the sitemap, internal links, retired routes, redirects and verification/utility endpoints. The audit found 29 sitemap URLs all returning HTTP 200, correct self-canonicals, matching language, no `noindex`, zero source-route collisions, zero active/tombstone collisions, zero broken internal URLs, zero orphaned articles, valid JSON-LD syntax and no invalid recorded `hreflang` edges. All 19 articles were reachable within two clicks from their own language homepage, but the article bodies had zero same-language article-to-article links. The new shared related-article section uses only the existing bilingual topic taxonomy; CI checks each generated suggestion against the same-language topic graph and limits it to three links. Seven English article descriptions exactly repeated `og:title`; this was detected after comparing the snippet description with the actual Open Graph title (Jekyll appends the site name to the HTML `<title>`). The expanded author-identity gate found no visible author links, missing BlogPosting author URLs (and Persian author labels falling back to English) on all 19 posts, plus no `ProfilePage` entity on either About page. These verified issues are fixed in the branch; the baseline report records them explicitly. The corrected report parser counts 13 English and 6 Persian articles and reads Jekyll Atom `href` attributes; see the JSON audit for the full per-URL evidence and response headers.
