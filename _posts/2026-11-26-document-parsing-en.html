In this article, we draw on practical experience processing Persian documents to understand why turning a PDF into reliable knowledge is much more than picking an AI model. The reported numbers and experimental results refer to limited, documented samples; educational examples and future ideas are distinguished from demonstrated results.

Part I: Why is reading a PDF so complicated?

1. A problem beyond text extraction

At first glance, the problem looks simple: we have a PDF, want to extract its text, and feed it into a RAG system or AI agent.

Install a few libraries, extract the text, split it into chunks, generate embeddings, and put them in a vector database.

But once we work with real Persian documents, especially financial, banking, legal, and organizational documents, we realize it is not that simple.

Imagine a financial document containing this table:

Loan type Interest rate Maximum amount
Purchase of goods 18% 500 million rials
Working capital 23% 2 billion rials
Qard al-hasan loan 4% 300 million rials

If a parser extracts every word and number from this table, has it done its job correctly?

Not necessarily.

All numbers might be read correctly, but the 23% interest rate could be assigned to the qard al-hasan loan. The word “million” might disappear, or 18 might become 81. Even if every bit of text is extracted correctly, the relationships between rows and columns could be lost.

In each case, incorrect information enters the database, and a powerful language model can generate a thoroughly convincing answer based on that incorrect information.

The language model is not necessarily at fault; the error starts as soon as the document enters the system.

This is one of the most important lessons in RAG engineering: errors in the input knowledge can severely limit the quality of the answers.

2. What is a PDF, really?

A common mistake is treating a PDF like a Word document or a plain text file. PDFs were primarily designed for displaying and printing documents, not necessarily for reconstructing their logical structure.

What a human sees as a heading, paragraph, or table might just be a collection of glyphs, lines, and coordinates inside the PDF. The parser needs to rediscover the relationships between those elements.

PDF type Characteristics Main challenge
Born-digital Digital text layer Reading order and structure
Scanned PDF Scanned page images Text recognition with OCR
Hybrid PDF Combination of text, images, and scans Selecting the right method for each region

A 100-page PDF might contain 95 digital text pages, three scanned pages, and two pages with image-based tables. Choosing a processing method for the entire file is not always appropriate. We might need to make the decision per page or even per small region.

3. OCR, layout analysis, and document parsing

These terms are often used interchangeably, even though they describe different responsibilities.

Stage Responsibility
OCR Recognizing letters, words, and numbers from images
Layout Analysis Identifying headings, paragraphs, tables, figures, footnotes, and formulas
Table Structure Recognition Detecting rows, columns, merged cells, and their relationships to headers
Reading Order Reconstructing the logical reading order of elements
Document Parsing Combining this information to reconstruct a coherent document structure

A model could be excellent at reading Persian text but unable to identify the table cell it belongs to, or the legal article to which a clause refers.

The world's best OCR is not necessarily the world's best document parser. Evaluating a document processing system solely on OCR accuracy is not enough.

Part II: Understanding the tools and architecture

4. What does PyMuPDF do?

PyMuPDF is one of the tools used in the architecture under study to access PDF content directly. Its uses include extracting digital text and glyph coordinates, rendering pages as images, identifying images, and inspecting the positions of page elements.

For example, if we want to know where a number appears on a page, we can retain information like this:

{
  "text": "۱۸٪",
  "page": 5,
  "bbox": [120, 340, 165, 360]
}

bbox or bounding box, is the rectangular area occupied by an element on the page. These coordinates allow the system to return to the actual location of that element later.

But PyMuPDF has limitations too. In one experiment on a challenging Persian page, 308 of 980 extracted characters belonged to the PUA range, roughly one-third.

PUA stands for Private Use Area, a Unicode range reserved for private use and custom mappings. Some fonts and PDFs may use such mappings, so the text can look perfectly correct to a human while the extracted characters do not have the intended meaning.

The lesson is not that PyMuPDF is a bad library; it is that direct PDF text extraction is not always the source of truth.

5. Docling and TableFormer

Docling is a document processing tool that helps analyze page structure and convert documents into structured representations. One of its components is TableFormer, which detects table structure.

In the implementation under study, Docling was configured as follows (a conceptual representation, not a complete executable code snippet):

do_table_structure = True
table_structure_mode = ACCURATE
do_cell_matching = True
do_ocr = False
Option Meaning
do_table_structure Table structure recognition
ACCURATE Mode focused on structure recognition accuracy
do_cell_matching Matching PDF text to detected cells
do_ocr OCR is disabled on this particular path

Even toggling do_cell_matching did not fix the corrupted Persian characters in the experiment on a difficult page. An image-based formula was not recognized as a formula, and the table structure was incomplete.

In this architecture, TableFormer is part of Docling, not an independent parser that reprocesses the entire document after Docling.

Selecting a mode ACCURATE does not guarantee real accuracy. Settings should be chosen based on actual experimental outputs, not merely their names.

The ultimate goal is not plain text, but a structured version of the document whose information and provenance can be checked. That is the difference between text extraction and structural reconstruction.

7. DocumentIR and TableIR: Why isn't Markdown enough?

Markdown is great for humans and language models to read, but has limitations when storing all the structural details of a document.

For example, in a complex table, a cell may span three rows. A table might continue on the next page, or a footnote might apply only to a particular column. Converting everything directly to Markdown can lose some of these relationships.

The architectural solution is an intermediate representation. DocumentIR retains the overall document structure, while TableIR retains the precise structure of tables.

A simple conceptual example of TableIR:

{
  "type": "table",
  "table_id": "table_001",
  "page": 5,
  "cells": [
    {
      "row": 1,
      "column": 2,
      "text": "۱۸٪",
      "header": "نرخ سود",
      "rowspan": 1,
      "colspan": 1,
      "bbox": [120, 340, 165, 360],
      "status": "REVIEW_REQUIRED"
    }
  ]
}

Now the system knows not only the 18% figure, but also its column, page, coordinates, and whether its accuracy has been verified.

Original document
        |
        v
DocumentIR + TableIR
(canonical representation)
        |
        +-- JSON
        +-- HTML
        +-- Markdown
        `-- RAG chunks

Maintain one structured source of truth and generate all other outputs from it.

If the original table is corrected, Markdown, HTML, and chunks should be regenerated from that same structure. Otherwise the JSON might be correct while an outdated, incorrect version remains in the vector database. This is a particularly misleading source of errors in real systems.

Part III: Why Persian documents are harder

8. The nightmare of RTL, Unicode, and Persian numerals

Persian is written right to left, but not every element in Persian text follows that direction. Numbers, some formulas, English words, dates, and mixed expressions behave differently.

Original value Possible incorrect output
۱۴۰۱ ۱۰۴۱
۹٬۰۳۹٬۰۰۰ ۰۰۰٬۹۳۰٬۹
+10 10+
≤ 20 < 20
۱۸٪ ۱۸
500 million rials 500 rials

These are not merely visual glitches. In financial documents, each can change the meaning of a rule or calculation.

Visual order and logical order

  • Visual Order: The order in which characters appear on the page.
  • Logical Order: The order in which characters should be stored in memory and processed as text.

The Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm, or BiDi, helps manage the display of text containing a mixture of directions.

A report about Docling's C++ engine suggested that right-to-left character handling caused some Persian numerals to be reversed. The exact upstream bug was not independently confirmed in the investigation under study, but similar errors were observed in real experiments.

Is reversing every number enough?

No; that can introduce new and even worse errors. Imagine that the parser extracted the number 21 . How can we tell whether the correct value was 21 or 12?

One approach tested was to first extract the native page text with PyMuPDF. If the value 12 appeared in the source but 21 did not, the system could cautiously correct the order. If both values appeared in the source, it should not guess.

This approach was useful in some samples but is insufficient for scanned PDFs or PDFs whose text layer is itself corrupted. Even finding the reversed value on the same page does not necessarily prove it belongs to the same cell.

Evidence-based correction is better than guesswork. But the evidence itself must also be checked for reliability.

9. The hardest table problems

The challenge with tables is not just reading cells; it is identifying the relationships between them.

Merged cells

Imagine the heading “installment sale” occupies a vertically merged cell spanning three rows:

Contract type Term Interest rate
Installment sale 12 months 18%
(same cell continued) 24 months 20%
(same cell continued) 36 months 23%

If the parser stores only the text of the second and third rows, their relationship to the contract type may disappear. TableIR must preserve the rowspan relationship.

Tables continued on the next page

Page 15                  Page 16
+-------+-------+        +-------+-------+
| Header A      |        | No header     |
+-------+-------+        +-------+-------+
| Row 1 | Value |        | Row 3 | Value |
| Row 2 | Value |  --->  | Row 4 | Value |
+-------+-------+        +-------+-------+

The second page might have no header but still inherit the previous page's headers. In one real sample, a possible cross-page connection was detected, but the final table was not accepted because of unverified cells and incomplete headers.

Adjacent tables with different meanings

Imagine a page with two independent tables about guarantee requirements and loan eligibility. Incorrectly merging them could assign guarantee requirements to loan eligibility, even if every number was read correctly.

Blank forms

In one real sample, a form had 18 rows and five columns, or 90 positions, but the parser reconstructed a much smaller structure. Empty cells do not mean the structure is unimportant.

Footnotes, table titles, units of measurement, rows split at page boundaries, and multilevel headers each introduce their own problems. That is why the percentage of correctly read words is not a good standalone measure of table quality.

Part IV: Vision models and information retrieval

10. Why use a vision-language model?

Imagine a PDF with corrupted native text, but everything is clearly visible when you open the page. We can extract the relevant region as an image and send it to a vision-language model such as Gemini or an open-source VLM.

But there is an important architectural decision: should we send the whole PDF to the model, or only the parts that need visual reading?

In one tested path, the VLM's responsibility was intentionally limited:

Page image
    |
    v
Select suspicious region
    |
    v
Crop + stable region ID
    |
    v
VLM transcription
    |
    v
Validate and reconcile with source

The model receives the image and returns something like this:

{
  "region_id": "page_5_region_12",
  "recognized_text": "نرخ ۱۸٪",
  "unreadable_spans": []
}

The model should not analyze, translate, summarize, or complete the information. It is not supposed to decide how many rows the table has or which column a number belongs to. When the geometry is known, those responsibilities belong to other pipeline components.

This architecture has several potential advantages: lower processing costs, replaceable models, traceable errors, and a narrower scope in which the model can invent or add information.

Of course, if the crop is wrong or the layout model fails to detect a region entirely, even the best VLM will never get a chance to read it.

This limited VLM role should not be confused with the separate path that delegates full-page structured extraction to a provider.

11. How do we know which part is corrupted?

This question may be more important than OCR itself. If the system could always detect which part it had misread, we could reprocess just that part with another method. But detecting errors is itself a difficult engineering problem.

Detection method What does it find?
Unicode Validation Corrupted or unexpected characters, such as PUA and �
Layout Overlap Text or images missing from the document structure
Table Validation Incomplete cells, rows, or headers
Empty Cell Inspection An empty cell with visible content in the image
Page Coverage Omitted or unprocessed pages
Numeric Cross-check Some conflicts in numbers and signs
Formula Detection Regions requiring formula detection or transcription

For example, if Docling identifies an empty cell, we can use its coordinates to check whether the region actually contains text, images, or drawn content.

But here's the important problem: if the true number is 18 and the parser returns 19 , both values are valid Unicode. Both can occupy a valid cell, and both can look semantically plausible.

As a result, many quality checks might find nothing wrong.

Failing to detect an error is not the same as proving correctness.

12. Does a confidence score solve the problem?

Suppose an OCR model tells us it read a number correctly with 98% confidence. Can we accept it directly?

One real experiment showed that an OCR model misread important signs and numbers even when its confidence was high.

Before relying on confidence scores, we need to calibrate them: use samples with trustworthy answers to examine how reported confidence relates to actual accuracy. Even then, model behavior may change on a new document, font, or image quality.

Confidence is a useful decision signal, not definitive proof of correctness.

Part V: Experiments and lessons learned

13. Comparing different parsers and models

Recorded results from limited experiments offer an interesting picture of the difficulty of the problem.

Tool or model Recorded result
PyMuPDF 308 PUA characters out of 980 characters on one difficult page
Docling / TableFormer Unicode issues, incomplete table structure, and a failed formula detection
Gemini Flash 3 Exact recognition of 17 out of 26 cell texts

Inkling 2 Mini Failed initial vision screening on a synthetic financial image

These were not identical tests under identical conditions, so they should not be turned into a definitive ranking.

Tools such as Marker, MinerU, Surya, PP-DocLayoutV3, PaddleOCR, and LlamaParse were also considered, but available evidence was insufficient to establish their relative superiority.

The important point is that a model can generate perfectly valid JSON while still containing incorrect text or extra cells.

Structured output guarantees adherence to a specified structure, not the correctness of the information inside it.

Inkling 2 Mini experiment

On a synthetic financial image, this model correctly extracted only three of five critical digits and neither of two complete financial expressions. It therefore failed the initial screening.

This does not establish that the model is poor at every vision task. It only shows that it did not provide sufficient evidence to advance to the next stage of that financial use case. Potential success in textual financial question answering does not imply success in OCR or vision.

Persian-specialized OCR experiment

One interesting experiment used the Bina Rizeh model on 37 real crops from four document families.

CER is character error rate and WER is word error rate. Lower is better for both. Some acceptance failures involved spacing and text boundaries, not necessarily financial semantic errors.

This experiment taught an important lesson: a model specialized for Persian is not necessarily suitable for Persian financial documents. Font, digits, formulas, mathematical symbols, scan quality, and document structure also matter.

Some sign errors also existed in the raw model output before RTL correction. In other words, the problem was not merely display direction; some errors came from recognition itself.

Part VI: Reliability engineering

14. Cache: friend or foe?

Caching is appealing in document processing. Processing a 100-page PDF may involve running several models and sending many API requests. If we have processed the document before, why pay the same cost again?

But one investigation found that running the parser with force=True did not necessarily mean re-extracting the document. The system could read the previous parser response from cache and rebuild only derived outputs.

Imagine changing the OCR model, fixing an important Docling bug, or writing a new prompt. You run the pipeline again and are happy with the result, but you are actually still looking at output from the previous model!

To solve this, four independent operations were defined:

Operation Responsibility
verify-only Inspect existing output
rebuild-derived Rebuild outputs from previously extracted data
reparse-regions Re-extract specified regions
reparse-document-fresh Actually re-extract the entire document

An idea for a better cache

Instead of using only the file name as the cache key, we can build cache identity from a combination of factors:

Cache Key = hash(
    Document Hash
  + Region Identity
  + Crop Hash
  + Model Version
  + Parser Configuration
  + Prompt Version
  + Schema Version
)

If any of these factors changes, the system must determine whether the old cache remains valid. Nor does changing one stage necessarily require rerunning all earlier stages. If only Markdown generation changed, visual extraction again makes little sense.

This approach can enable incremental processing and reduce costs for large documents.

15. Provenance: Where did the information come from?

Imagine an AI agent telling a user that the maximum loan amount is two billion rials. If the user wants to verify the source, the system must be able to return to its exact location in the original document.

Provenance means recording the origin and production history of information.

Source Document (identity + hash)
              |
              v
Page (physical + printed number)
              |
              v
Region (bounding box + crop)
              |
              v
Table / Cell (row + column)
              |
              v
Extracted Value (method + status)
              |
              v
Chunk / Retrieval Evidence
              |
              v
AI Answer + Citation

In one sample, the eighth physical PDF page corresponded to the seventh printed page. If the system ignores that difference, the citation may be wrong from the start.

Besides building trust, provenance is crucial for debugging. When we find an incorrect number, we must know whether the PDF decoder introduced it, OCR misread it, the table parser moved it, or chunking lost its meaning.

16. Quarantine: The art of refusing suspicious information

A reliable system should not be forced to produce a complete result for every input. Sometimes the best response is to admit that part of the document could not be processed confidently.

Status Meaning
ACCEPTED Content passed the defined acceptance criteria.
REVIEW_REQUIRED Content was extracted but still requires review.
QUARANTINED Content is incomplete, invalid, or suspicious and must not be published as reliable knowledge.

In one historical report, 231 regions requiring visual processing had been detected:

Status Count
Stored response, review required 34
No matching response 197
Accepted Zero

The problem was not merely that some regions had not been extracted. More importantly, the system needed to report accurately what it did not know.

Silently removing a suspicious table from the output can make a document appear clean while leaving it incomplete.

The system must both prevent publication of incorrect information and report missing information.

17. Problems unrelated to the AI model itself

When building AI systems, we often focus heavily on model choice. But many recorded problems were software engineering problems.

For example, in one incident the model produced a successful response containing 32 processed regions. Validation then failed and the extracted response was lost. The model had done its job, but the storage architecture was inadequate.

The solution was to persist the raw model response before validation so it could later be inspected or replayed.

Other issues included concurrent duplicate requests, validating files by extension rather than actual content, stale chunks, mismatched parser output and index, and incorrectly reporting success for partially processed documents.

None of these issues is solved by swapping Gemini for Qwen.

A document parsing service needs backend engineering, job management, durable storage, idempotency, observability, and publication lifecycle control as well as AI.

Imagine hundreds of unit tests passing successfully. That's good news, but what does it prove?

It proves that the behaviors for which we wrote tests work under the specified conditions. It does not prove that a new financial document with a different font, complex formula, and multipage table will be processed correctly.

The audit under study covered nine PDFs totaling 121 pages, but independent page review had not been completed. So despite numerous engineering fixes, the system still did not meet the final acceptance criteria.

This is one of the most important differences between a successful prototype and a reliable product.

Part VIII: How can we build a better parser?

20. Benchmark the entire pipeline rather than the model

Imagine building a specialized Persian document processing system. The first question is often whether Gemini is better than Qwen or Docling is better than Marker.

But perhaps we should change the question:

What is the best combination of document processing components for our target document types?

Each stage can be tested independently.

Stage Things to compare
Native Extraction PyMuPDF and other PDF backends
Layout Detection Docling, Surya, PP-DocLayout
Image Preprocessing Resolution changes, deskew, denoising, contrast
OCR PaddleOCR, Persian-specialized models, and other OCR systems
Vision Recovery Local and API-based VLMs
Table Recognition Table geometry and structure reconstruction models
Reading Order Reading order algorithms and models
Reconciliation Methods for aligning text, images, and structure
Serialization JSON, HTML, Markdown
Quality Control Structural rules, source comparison, independent review

Experiments may reveal that a stage is unnecessary. A cheaper model may perform better and more consistently at one stage. Even combining two powerful models may produce worse results than a simple model, because errors from the first propagate into the second.

What is an ablation study?

Ablation means removing or changing a system component and measuring its effect on the output.

Proposed experiment Combination
A Docling
B Docling + OCR
C Docling + VLM
D Docling + OCR + VLM
E Alternative layout model + OCR

All experiments should use the same documents, metrics, and fixed ground truth. Wherever possible, change only one variable per comparison.

This lets us understand how much quality each component contributes and whether the cost and complexity are justified.

21. Gold dataset: The most important asset of a specialized system

To evaluate OCR or a parser, we need to know the correct answer. But who decides what that is?

If one language model reviews another model's output, both may make the same mistake. If we use the previous parser's output as reference, we may import its old errors into the benchmark.

A gold dataset is a collection of inputs and correct, independently reviewed outputs.

For Persian documents, this collection should go beyond simple text. It should include complex tables, blank forms, formulas, stamps, low-quality scans, mixed English and Persian text, numbers, percentages, footnotes, and multicolumn documents.

For each sample, we can retain information like this:

{
  "sample_id": "financial_cell_001",
  "expected_text": "≤ ۱۸٪",
  "element_type": "table_cell",
  "critical_tokens": ["≤", "۱۸", "٪"],
  "reference_status": "VERIFIED"
}

A gold dataset is not just for model selection. After every change to the parser, OCR, prompt, or RTL algorithm, it can help prevent old errors from returning.

That is why building a precise, high-quality Persian dataset may be more valuable than building a new pipeline.

Part IX: Ideas to explore and experiment with

So far, we have discussed recorded problems and experiences. The following are research proposals; their superiority has not been demonstrated in the experiments under study.

Idea 1: Adaptive document parsing

Instead of sending every document down a fixed path, first identify the nature of each page or region.

Input page / region
         |
         v
  Region analysis
         |
         +-- Digital text --> Native extraction
         +-- Scanned text --> OCR
         `-- Difficult region --> VLM
         |
         v
  Reconstruct and verify

Process clean digital text directly, send scans to OCR, and inspect complex or suspicious regions with a VLM.

Potential benefits are reduced cost and faster processing. The drawback is that the routing system itself can make mistakes.

Research question: Are routing savings worth its complexity and potential errors?

Idea 2: Multi-engine consensus

Why always rely on one extraction engine? Imagine a sensitive region read by three different engines:

Engine Output
Native PDF 18%
OCR 18%
VLM 81%

Disagreement between outputs can itself signal the need for review.

Of course, a majority vote is not truth. Two engines might use the same backend or text mapping and make the same mistake.

The goal of this architecture should be conflict detection, not automatic acceptance of the majority vote.

Idea 3: Adaptive-resolution OCR

Sending every page at very high resolution can increase processing cost and memory use. Low resolution, on the other hand, can erase dots, percent signs, fine text, and formula details.

We could process the page at ordinary resolution first and generate a more precise crop or higher-DPI render only for difficult regions.

An interesting benchmark would compare extraction quality, processing time, and cost across resolutions.

Idea 4: Cell-level verification

Instead of declaring an entire table correct or incorrect, maintain an independent status for each cell.

Value Hypothetical status
18% Verified
500 million rials Verified
24 months Review required
23% Conflict

This lets us identify exactly which parts of the table require reprocessing. But accepting every cell does not necessarily mean their relationships are correct.

Verification is therefore necessary at both value and structure levels.

Idea 5: Building a synthetic Persian dataset

Using HTML, Persian fonts, and PDF generation tools, we could create thousands of synthetic documents with answers known from the outset.

For example, generate tables with random numbers, merged cells, Persian dates, mixed English text, and various formulas, then create scanned or noisy versions.

This could help with regression testing, model training, and identifying error patterns.

The limitation is that synthetic documents may not fully reproduce the complexity, font corruption, and strange behavior of real PDFs. Real data is needed alongside them.

Idea 6: Human-in-the-loop as part of the product

Build a review interface showing the original document image on the right and its extracted structure on the left. Clicking a cell highlights its exact location on the source. Users can correct values, verify structure, and record the reason for errors.

This is not just a debugging tool; it can become a gold dataset creation tool.

Over time, human corrections can generate valuable data for model evaluation, general rules, and even fine-tuning.

Idea 7: Document parsing as an independent product

Imagine an API service designed to accept various Persian document types:

Inputs:
  PDF / Scanned PDF / DOCX / Image

Outputs:
  Structured JSON
  HTML
  Markdown
  DocumentIR / TableIR
  Quality Report
  Source Provenance

Developers would no longer need to solve Persian PDF processing from scratch for every RAG system. Financial, legal, insurance, educational, and other organizations could process their documents and receive outputs suited to AI agents.

Such a product's competitive advantage need not be a proprietary OCR model. It could come from a precise pipeline, trustworthy experiments, sound Persian processing engineering, and reliable output structure.

Part X: The biggest technical obstacles

If we summarize the problems into a few fundamental obstacles, these six stand out.

Obstacle Why it matters
No trustworthy gold dataset Actual quality cannot be measured properly.
Hidden semantic errors Data looks valid but has the wrong meaning.
Table structure reconstruction Reading text without its relationships is insufficient.
Detecting missing regions The system might not even notice omitted content.
No validated local alternative for vision Independence, cost, and infrastructure control remain challenging.
No independent end-to-end evaluation Success of individual components does not guarantee success of the entire system.

A proposed research sequence is to define acceptance criteria and a gold dataset first, then compare pipeline components, address root causes, and finally reprocess and evaluate the entire document collection using the chosen architecture.

Choosing a final model before establishing this evidence would be premature.

Conclusion: What we need to learn as AI engineers

The most important lesson from this experience is that document processing is not a problem of one AI model; it is a problem of designing a complete system.

In that system, the PDF decoder, layout model, OCR, VLM, structure reconstruction algorithms, storage, cache, quality control, and evaluation all contribute to the final result.

AI engineering is not just choosing the most powerful model. Sometimes a simple library with a limited responsibility, a well-designed schema, durable raw responses, cell coordinates, or preventing publication of incomplete data has more impact than upgrading a model.

Nor should we mistake complexity for progress. Adding models, agents, recovery layers, and algorithms is worthwhile only when we can demonstrate that they solve a real problem.

The ultimate goal should be the simplest architecture capable of demonstrating the required quality with reproducible evidence.

A great parser does not just read documents; it can show what it read, where it read it, what it could not read, and why its output deserves trust.
